---
title: How to Add a Voiceover to a Screen Recording on a Mac
date: 2026-11-12
excerpt: A practical way to narrate a screen recording on a Mac. Prepare a few notes, record while the video plays, clean up the takes on a timeline, and export one finished MP4.
---

A screen recording explains a lot more with a voice on top. The usual way to add one is to record your voice in one app, drag the file into a video editor, nudge it into place, and start over every time you stumble on a sentence.

This guide shows a faster way. You record while you watch the video, fix mistakes on a timeline, and export one finished MP4. The steps use Voximov, but the advice before and after recording works with any tool.

## Before you record

- **Cut the video first.** Trim the screen recording before you narrate it. Narration timed to the final cut rarely needs to move.
- **Write notes, not a speech.** A few bullet points for each scene are enough. Most people sound stiff reading full sentences and natural talking from notes.
- **Wear headphones.** You will hear the video while you talk. Without headphones, the microphone records the video's sound along with your voice.
- **Find a quiet room with soft surfaces.** A room with curtains, a rug and a sofa sounds better than an empty kitchen. A USB microphone helps, but a quiet room matters more.

## Record over the video

1. Open Voximov and import the video from the menu at the top left, or drop it on the window. The video gets its own track.
2. Move the playhead to where the first line should start.
3. Press **R**. The video plays and Voximov records your voice on the armed audio track. Press **R** again to stop.
4. Record in short takes, one scene or one idea at a time. If you stumble, stop, move the playhead back, and record that part again.

Short takes make editing easy. Each take is its own clip that you can move, trim or delete without touching the others.

## Clean up the takes

- Press **S** to split every track at the playhead, then select the part you do not want and delete it.
- Drag a clip to move it, or drag its edges to trim it.
- Copy and paste a section to reuse it, and undo any edit you regret.

Your original video and recordings never change. The project file keeps the edits, so you can come back later and change your mind.

## Make the voice sound finished

Raw microphone recordings usually sound thin, uneven or boomy. In the **Effects** tab, start from a voice preset. A high-pass filter removes low rumble, an equalizer shapes the tone, and a compressor brings loud and quiet words closer together.

Voximov also normalizes the loudness of the export to −16 LUFS with a −1.5 dBTP peak ceiling by default, a common level for spoken content online. Your videos then come out at the same volume, whatever your microphone level was that day.

In the **Mixer** tab, lower the video's own sound under your voice, or mute the video track to leave it out of the export. If you add music on another audio track, keep it well below the voice.

## Export

Export the video as an MP4. Voximov cuts the video to match your edits, mixes all tracks, and encodes the result with the Mac's hardware H.264 encoder. If you edit the picture somewhere else, export the mix as WAV or MP3 instead.

## No time to record? Type it

If you would rather not record your own voice, use a script clip. Choose the **Script** tool, drag across an audio track, type the line and press Enter. An AI voice speaks it in place, running on your Mac with nothing uploaded. The clip's width is the time the line may take, and Voximov shows you when the speech runs longer than that.

Whichever way you narrate, the workflow stays the same. Finish the picture, add the voice in short pieces, clean it up, and export once.
